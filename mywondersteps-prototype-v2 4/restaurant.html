One step in Barcelona

A doorway, a noisy neighborhood, and one memorable bite

Not a restaurant review—just a small moment I wanted to keep from a walk through Barcelona.

Barcelona, SpainSummer 2026La Cova Fumada

Walking through the neighborhoods of Barcelona, surrounded by locals talking, arguing, and smiling, we came across a small restaurant tucked into an old building. The entrance was easy to miss, with its rustic wooden doors and a modest sign above, but inside it felt lively and authentic.

We later learned that its signature potato dish, La Ramala, was invented here. As we looked through the menu, it was listed right at the top. When it arrived, it came as a small, round, golden-brown ball. With just a light press of the spoon, it opened easily.

The taste I remember

La Ramala

Rich and savory at first, then followed by a strong chili kick. Inside, it was smooth and slightly saucy, almost melting in the mouth.

What stayed with me was not only the dish. It was the whole small scene: the worn wooden doorway, the voices from inside, the people sharing tables, and the feeling that we had briefly stepped into someone else’s everyday Barcelona.

A small step, saved before it disappears into the rest of the journey.
